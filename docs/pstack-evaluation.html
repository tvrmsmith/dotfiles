pstack, evaluated against your setup

backnotprop/pstack, a mirror of the Cursor plugin cursor/plugins/pstack by poteto (React core team, ex Meta/Netflix, now Cursor). MIT licensed. 13 workflow skills, 19 principle skills, 14 playbooks, 1 subagent. Evaluated 2026-08-19 at commit bf5378a.

Verdict: cherry-pick, do not install the plugin.

pstack is written for Cursor, and the coupling is not cosmetic. Its flagship skills spawn four named frontier models per run (gpt-5.3-codex-high-fast, composer-2.5-fast). Claude Code's Agent tool accepts sonnet, opus, haiku, and fable. The multi-model diversity that makes interrogate, arena, and how --critique work does not survive the port.

The 19 principle skills are pure prose with zero tool coupling. They port perfectly, they are the best thing in the repo, and they are the piece your setup is actually missing. Take those. Take show-me-your-work. Adapt two or three others later if you feel the gap. Leave poteto-mode alone, because it contradicts three of your standing rules.

Where it contradicts your CLAUDE.md

Never block on the human. pstack's delegation principle says proceed on anything reversible and let the human course-correct after the fact. Your rule says "confirm decisions with me before acting: surface the choice, recommend, wait for my explicit answer." These are opposites, and poteto-mode cites the pstack version as a non-negotiable. Installing it puts two contradictory instructions in the same context window, which is how you get an agent that picks one at random.

Todolists. Every pstack playbook opens with "open a todolist and copy the steps in verbatim." Your beads block prohibits TodoWrite entirely and routes all task tracking to bd. The playbooks are salvageable, but every step-tracking instruction has to be rewritten to bd create.

Planning. poteto says "i don't believe in planning, the best spec is code." Your rule sends creative and design work to /wayfinder and /grill-with-docs before implementation. Not a hard conflict, but the two philosophies pull in different directions on the same tasks.

The seven to leave out

  • never-block-on-the-human. Contradicts your confirm-first rule. Leaving it out is the point.
  • guard-the-context-window. Your delegation section already says this, in more detail, with a model rubric.
  • type-system-discipline. Overlaps coding-standards. Merge the good lines instead.
  • experience-first, exhaust-the-design-space. Weak against your defaults. You already obsess over UI polish and reach for prototypes.
  • outcome-oriented-execution, migrate-callers-then-delete-legacy-apis. Narrow to planned multi-phase rewrites. Add them if you start one.

How to install what you take

One structural recommendation first. Do not symlink 12 principle skills into dot-claude/skills/. Twelve skill descriptions sit in your context on every turn of every session, roughly 500 to 800 tokens, whether or not any of them fire. Twelve pointers for material that is really one flat rule set is the wrong shape. Collapse them into one reference file that your CLAUDE.md points at with a single line.

Step 1. Vendor the whole repo, expose a slice

Extend the entry you already added, so vendor/update pulls the full skills tree. Disk is free, context is not. Symlink only what you expose.

GIT_SKILLS=(
  ...
  "pstack|backnotprop-pstack|main|skills|https://github.com/backnotprop/pstack.git"
)

Note the tradeoff: fetch_git_skill does rm -rf on the destination before extracting, so any local edit you make under vendor/pstack/skills/ is destroyed on the next update. Keep your adaptations in dot-claude/skills/ as real files, and treat the vendor tree as read-only upstream.

Step 2. Symlink the two skills that port clean

ln -sfn ../../vendor/pstack/skills/unslop            dot-claude/skills/unslop  # done
ln -sfn ../../vendor/pstack/skills/show-me-your-work dot-claude/skills/show-me-your-work

Step 3. Write one principles skill, not twelve

A single dot-claude/skills/engineering-principles/SKILL.md, one heading per principle, roughly two to four lines each, condensed from the 12 upstream files. One description in context instead of twelve. Add one line to your Engineering Standards section pointing at it. Cost: one line of always-loaded context, versus twelve.

Step 4. Stop there

Run for a couple of weeks. If you find yourself wanting a decision-quality gate on a design before you write code, come back for architect and arena together, and budget real time to rewrite the model tables and the Task call shape. That is the only adaptation in this repo I would call clearly worth the effort, and only after the principles have proven themselves.

Two things I would push back on

The principles are load-bearing for the whole repo, and pstack knows it. poteto-mode opens by demanding the agent read all 19 and then cite, by name, which principle changed which decision. That citation requirement is the actual innovation here, more than any individual playbook. If you take the principles without any mechanism forcing the agent to cite them against real choices, you get 12 more rules the agent nods at and ignores. Worth stealing the citation demand along with the content.

The parallelism story is weaker than the README claims for you specifically. "Fearless parallelism" in pstack means several models attacking one problem and a judge picking a winner. Yours means several agents attacking different beads in different worktrees. Those are different shapes. pstack's fan-out skills solve a problem you mostly do not have, which is why they cluster in the adapt and skip columns despite being the repo's showcase.
